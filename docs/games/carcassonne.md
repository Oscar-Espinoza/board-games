# Carcassonne — Big Box 3.1

Carcassonne is a 2–6 player, authoritative tile-placement game in this catalog.
Create a room, invite friends, and let the host start. The game then opens its own
setup: choose Learn, Base, Expanded Classic, Full Big Box, or individual modules.
The room host controls this shared setup while every player sees it update live and
confirms readiness. A configuration change clears confirmations. Host authority
transfers with room ownership. The starting seat defaults to the first player; the
host can choose another visible player chip. Seat order remains fixed for the match.

The eleven selectable modules are River, Abbot, Inns & Cathedrals, Traders &
Builders, Flying Machines, Messages, Ferries, Gold Mines, Magicians, Robbers, and
Crop Circles. Expanded Classic enables the first four plus farmers. Learn disables
farmers. Full Big Box is a long game (the booklet estimates 100–120 minutes).
There are no competitive bots, online solo variants, accounts or persistence.

## Try it without a group

The catalog's **Try it on your own** link opens `/practice/carcassonne`. You control
three seats and the view automatically follows the next required decision maker,
including other players' pig, marker and crop-circle choices. The default learning
table ends after 18 draws. Choose **Full match** for the ordinary deck and setup.
The selector also offers focused builder, pig, flight, ferry, gold, magic, message
and crop-circle fixtures. One **Start practice** action confirms the local setup for
all three simulated seats. Reset starts a fresh local table. None of these fixtures
or shortened decks is used by the server or imported by catalog metadata.

**Painted landscape · 6 seats** opens a deterministic, playable 29-tile table for
reviewing the redesign. Link directly with `/practice/carcassonne?scenario=table`.
The separate `/practice/carcassonne/artwork` inventory shows all 138 tile designs
at four rotations using the production artwork renderer.

## Play and controls

Select a highlighted frontier square, rotate the tile, and confirm its placement.
All neighboring edges must match. A red preview explains a mismatch. Legal moves
are generated by the same queries used for server validation. An inconvenient
but legal draw cannot be rejected. The server tries every frontier cell and all
four rotations before discarding an unplaceable tile.

Drag empty space to pan, use the wheel or a two-finger pinch to zoom, or use the
zoom buttons, Fit landscape and Last move controls at the bottom right (also
available in Settings). The angled landscape fills the screen, with player plaques
in a left column and your tile and individual meeples in a bottom tray. Compact
screens use a horizontally scrollable player strip and an expandable piece supply.
Picking up the tile reveals legal spaces;
drop it to preview, rotate it in place and confirm. Invalid drops return to the
hand. Settings also contains practice scenarios/reset, language, help and history. Zoom remains
anchored to the pointer or pinch midpoint. On the focused map, arrows cycle legal
locations, R/Shift+R rotate, Enter confirms, and +/- zoom. Space-drag and
Shift+arrows pan without selecting a tile; directional buttons offer the same
camera movements. A movement threshold
prevents a pan from becoming a tile selection. Consequential choices also have
ordinary keyboard-focusable buttons.

Select a region marker to inspect its connected feature, owners, strength, open
ends and provisional score. Pick up a physical meeple from your hand to reveal its legal region targets, then drag it onto one. Tab and Enter provide equivalent placement. A standard
meeple cannot join an occupied connected feature; flying and Crop Circles provide
their specified exceptions. Farmers remain until final scoring unless an expansion
explicitly returns them. The journal links accepted actions and scores to the map.
Scoring updates the seats around the screen and briefly shows a point receipt.

Local painted WebP materials supply the wooden table, meadows, town roofs, earth,
abbeys and keeps. SVG still defines every tile's roads, walls, rivers, regions and
module symbols; decoration cannot change topology. Numbered wooden meeples
supplement player colors; farmers
lie down and large meeples have a larger silhouette. Attached figures remain
linked to their meeple. English and Spanish text use Paraglide.

Brass guide, overview and settings controls sit in the upper-right header.
Hovering or focusing a physical figure explains its purpose. The field guide covers
the three-step turn, ordinary and final scoring, every player figure, and all eleven
modules; enabled rules appear first and are marked for the current game. Setup uses
the same guide language on illustrated module cards instead of native checkboxes.

A chart control beside the guide opens the live board overview during play.
It compares public scores, leader gaps, placed tiles, figure supplies and active
expansion resources for every seat. Its Mine, Rivals and Unclaimed watchlists rank
unfinished features by their provisional final value, then by open ends. Selecting
one returns to the landscape, focuses that feature and opens the ordinary inspection
panel. These summaries are derived only from the public player view; they do not
expose deck order, tile odds or suggested moves, and reset with the match.

Sound unlocks through a user gesture and has a remembered mute switch. Rotation
uses only a paper slide; tile placement uses a low wooden impact; meeples use a
smaller wooden tap; scoring uses a sequence of soft taps. No pitched oscillators
or electronic beeps are used. Gold, trade goods, flying, ferries, magic,
messages and victory have separate cues. Missing or denied audio never blocks
an action. Reduced-motion preferences disable placement and receipt animations.
Fresh/reconnected snapshots establish a silent event baseline.

## Rules and source record

Baseline: [English Big Box 3.1 booklet](https://cundco.de/media/26/95/90/1773929268/CC_BigBox_3.1_rule.pdf?ts=1773929331),
with the [official versioned rules index](https://cundco.de/spielregeln/cc-grundspiel-erweiterungen)
as the recovery location. The English booklet was retrieved during implementation;
appendix pages 22–23 were rendered and inspected. The planning record contains the
Spanish cross-check and further source links. Art is original; the publisher's
scanned tile art is not an application asset.

`tiles.ts` transcribes 72 base tiles, 12 river tiles, 24 Inns tiles, 24 Traders tiles,
8 each for Flight/Ferries/Gold/Magic/Robbers, and 6 Crop Circle tiles: 178 landscape
tiles with every module enabled. Messages are eight separate non-landscape cards.
Eight garden-bearing base copies have distinct IDs. Module river tiles stay out of
the ordinary deck when River is disabled. River uses its source initially and its
lake after the shuffled middle tiles; the usual base starting tile joins the deck.
Internal IDs use A–X for base, F1–F12 for River, E-prefixed Inns and H-prefixed
Traders; mini-expansions use descriptive prefixes and appendix letter order.

Important 3.1 behavior has focused regression coverage:

- Unfinished inn roads and cathedral cities retain ordinary final points (pp. 10–11).
- A builder is attached during deployment, returns when its existing feature is
  extended by its owner, and grants an extra turn. Ferry movement alone cannot
  trigger it (pp. 13, 17).
- A shield-bearing city completion triggers all pigs. They score three per distinct
  completed neighboring city, without a majority requirement; the pig returns and
  its owner may return the attached farmer. Pigs have no final multiplier (p. 14).
- Goods go to the completing player, independently of city ownership (p. 12).
- Flying fixes its server-generated die before landing selection, permits occupied
  incomplete roads/cities/monasteries, and has no fallback deployment after a miss
  (p. 15).
- Messages implement eight separate numbered actions. Their provisional scoring
  does not complete a feature or return its figures except message 8 (p. 16).
- Ferry road components are recomputed, so roads can split, reopen and score again
  with newly placed meeples. Short internal roads on Inns V are separate features
  (pp. 11, 17).
- Gold is assigned to tiles. Completion order is selected when gold is involved;
  tied owners collect individual ingots clockwise from the active seat. Unclaimed
  map gold is excluded at final scoring (p. 18).
- Mage adds distinct tiles; witch halves the modified score, rounded up (p. 19).
- Robbers use physical spaces modulo 50, never deduct from victims, and cannot
  trigger another robbery through their own reward (p. 20).
- Crop Circles resolve starting to the active player's left, with that player last;
  removing a meeple also returns its attached figure (p. 21).

### Resolution order and explicit app conventions

The page-24 sequence is implemented as resumable stages: pre-placement robbers,
placement, gold/magic effects, deployment or recall, ferry placement/repositioning,
completed features and goods, pigs, score-marker choices, robber rewards, message,
Crop Circles, extra turn or next player, then final scoring when no tile remains.
No stage waits for animation completion. Pending choices store their actor, prompt
identity and legal options; reconnecting does not regenerate dice or choices.

The following are deterministic app conventions for combinations not completely
specified by the booklet's broad timing chart. They should not be treated as
independent publisher rulings:

- All geometrically completed features are resolved before pig decisions; each pig
  scores once and sees every currently completed neighboring city. Gold ownership
  is resolved with its selected completed feature, before marker movement; gold
  does not itself advance a marker until final scoring.
- Ordinary awards and pig/abbot awards form each player's turn total. A player
  chooses one marker for that total. Robber awards use separate provenance and
  follow moved markers without generating robbery chains. Message awards use the
  same marker, allow no second message, and can trigger a still-waiting robber;
  that reward uses the victim's accumulated turn points.
- Builder and extra-turn-message awards are queued independently. Every extra
  turn is a new draw/turn with its own message allowance. Crop Circles settle
  before drawing that extra tile. A last-tile extra turn has no tile to draw and
  proceeds to final scoring after its pending decisions finish.
- A goods category with no collected token awards nobody ten points. A required
  action with no legal target is skipped; magic removes a figure when relocation
  has no legal destination. Zero-point awards do not move a marker or draw a message.

The handwritten topology and original geometry still merit independent tabletop
review, especially unusual enclosed fields and multi-expansion corner cases.
Automated self-consistency checks and seeded matches do not constitute an
independent verification against every printed tile.

## Architecture and evidence

Rules are pure TypeScript. `features.ts` rebuilds connected components using sparse
integer coordinates, rotated exterior ports and separate meadow half-edges. It
counts distinct tiles and completed city identities. Ferries add only their selected
local road connection; there is no merge-only cache or permanent road-scored flag.
`index.ts` validates intentions on a clone and exposes an explicit projection.
Future deck/message order never enters recipient views. Rooms retain the ordinary
revision checks, action-ID deduplication, socket-bound identity and replacement
credentials. The optional game-contract `presence` hook protects unconsumed setup
readiness without changing an in-progress decision. Readiness alone permits a
bounded stale-revision retry for the same setup and original request ID.

See [validation](../validation.md) for actual executed checks and limitations.
Physical Safari, native device audio/touch quality, and a real group Full Big Box
playtest remain separate human validation; browser automation cannot certify them.
